[00:00] What if all of Earth's continents joined into one giant landmass?

[00:05] It's happened before: Pangaea joined nearly all land until about two hundred million years ago.

[00:12] South America and Africa still fit like puzzle pieces, and plates drift about as fast as fingernails grow.

[00:21] One landmass changes the weather. Most moisture rises from the sea, so a supercontinent's heart would become a vast desert.

[00:31] People would crowd the rainy coasts, battered by monsoons. Inland, days would scorch and nights would freeze.

[00:40] Animals from every continent would meet, and not all would survive. When the Americas joined, many South American mammals vanished.

[00:51] At sea, there'd be far less coastline, and fewer shallow, sunlit waters where coral reefs grow.

[00:59] The next supercontinent may be brutal: one model finds only eight to sixteen percent of its land livable for mammals like us.
