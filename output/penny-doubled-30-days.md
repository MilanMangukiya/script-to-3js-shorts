# What if a penny doubled every day for 30 days?

- **Slug:** `penny-doubled-30-days`
- **Duration:** 47s
- **Core Question:** If you took one penny that doubles every day for 30 days instead of a million dollars, what would you actually end up with?

## youtube_shorts
**Title:** What if a penny doubled every day for 30 days?

You just turned down one million dollars for a single penny that doubles every day for thirty days. In this 47-second 3D science thought experiment, two weeks in you have $81.92, and by day 19 the pile outweighs a horse and snaps your kitchen table.

Day 20 is over a tonne of pennies worth only $5,242.88. Three days later, ten tons go through the floor. On day 28 you finally beat the million; two more doublings burst your house, and you end with $5,368,709.12, half of it added on the very last day. The million you refused would have fit in a briefcase.

Your floor gives out on day 23, but the penny doesn't beat the million until day 28. Which day would you have cashed out?

Scope: Thought experiment: you refuse $1,000,000 and take one US cent that doubles at the start of each day for 30 days (day 1 = 1 cent, day 30 = 2^29 cents = $5,368,709.12), kept as real pennies (2.5 g, 19.05 mm across, 1.52 mm thick; US Mint). Pile sizes assume loose pennies fill about 60% of their heap at a 24 degree slope; packing between 50% and 70% moves the house dates by less than a day. Day 19's 655 kg outweighs a typical riding horse (about 500 kg) and is several times the roughly 100 kg load used to test domestic tables; day 23's 10.5 tonnes is several times the 40 psf (about 195 kg per square metre) design load of a US home's floor. Those failures are plausible illustrations, not engineering predictions. Day 30 alone adds $2,684,354.56, half of the total. $1,000,000 in $100 bills is 10,000 notes of about 1 g each, roughly 10 kg. The pennies are copper-plated zinc; the kitchen, house and street are illustrative.
