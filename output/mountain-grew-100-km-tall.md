# What if a mountain on Earth grew 100 kilometres tall?

- **Slug:** `mountain-grew-100-km-tall`
- **Duration:** 55s
- **Core Question:** What would a single 100 km mountain look like and do — from a city street to the edge of space — and could Earth even hold it up?

## youtube_shorts
**Title:** What if a mountain on Earth grew 100 km tall?

What if one mountain on Earth grew 100 kilometres tall? That's eleven Everests stacked, with its summit at the edge of space.

In this 3D science simulation we climb it by altitude. At 9 km you pass Everest, breathing a third of the oxygen. Near 19 km (the Armstrong limit) water boils at body temperature, even the saliva on your tongue. By 30 km the noon sky is nearly black, and at 80 km meteors burn up beside you. Over 90% of its height is a death zone, and it stands about nine times taller than the weather, splitting storms and leaving a desert in its rain shadow.

The twist: Earth can't hold it up. The rock under the summit would carry about 2.6 GPa, roughly 26,000 atmospheres, more than ten times what granite can bear. The base would crush, flow and sink, which is why Earth's mountains top out near Everest's height.

Where would you be standing when it rose? 👇

A thought experiment: the growth, the meteor rate, the rain-shadow ratio and the 100,000-year collapse are illustrative, and numbers are rounded.
Sources: Britannica (Mount Everest); FAI (Kármán line statement); NASA NTRS (U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976); NOAA (layers of the atmosphere); National Geographic Education (rain shadow); UCAR (cloud types).
