# CLAUDE.md — What-If Shorts Studio: Claude Code Production Contract

## Mission and authority
Build and render `stories/<slug>.json` as a finished, cinematic Three.js documentary MP4. Preserve scientific accuracy, physical/illustrative distinctions and original visual storytelling. Treat `storyboard.schema.json`, the story JSON and these instructions as the contract; don't stop at scaffolding. **45–90 seconds inclusive**, with no padding simply to reach the upper bound.

## Persistent studio memory: mandatory asset reuse
The repo is a **growing production studio**, not one independent project per video. Its accumulated 3D assets must be maintained and reused.

**Before coding each video:**
1. Read `assets/registry.json` (small machine index) and only those registered source files whose IDs/tags match the storyboard. `ASSET_LIBRARY.md` is a human-readable index, not the source of truth. Read `asset_plan` in the storyboard; verify candidate reuse IDs against the registry; never assume candidate assets exist.
2. Prefer an existing asset factory, material, effect, lighting rig or system with **new configuration parameters** over rebuilding it. Reuse as much as visually appropriate; do not let reuse degrade the video or falsify its scientific model.
3. Shared **code** lives in `src/library/` (planet components, PBR material factories, ocean shaders, environment props, camera rigs, caption motions, render helpers); reusable **files** live in `assets/library/` (textures, models, audio, etc.). Story-specific compositions live separately in the existing scene/project structure. Never bake hard-coded storyboard timestamps or camera poses into generic reusable assets.
4. When an original creation will plausibly be useful in future videos, **parameterize and promote it to the library on that same render**. Expose typed configuration (e.g. planet radius/tilt/clouds, coast wave shape, star seed, lighting/color) and deterministic `time_s`; no hidden module-global side effects. Register it in `assets/registry.json` with stable ID, source path, description, tags, configurable parameters, `created_for`, `used_in`, original/license-cleared provenance and usage notes. Only register code/files that actually exist and render successfully; proposed candidates are NOT completed assets.
5. Track reused versus new asset IDs precisely in `metadata/<slug>.json`. The finalization script updates every registered item's `used_in` and rebuilds `ASSET_LIBRARY.md`. Never overwrite a shared asset in a way that breaks prior videos; add backwards-compatible parameters or version the asset when needed.

Recommended registry record shape (append to `assets/registry.json` `assets` array):
```json
{"id":"earth-atmosphere-v1","name":"Earth with atmosphere","type":"three_component","source_path":"src/library/planet/Earth.ts","description":"Configurable Earth component","tags":["earth","space","clouds"],"configurable_parameters":["radius","cloudOpacity","time_s"],"created_for":"first-video-slug","used_in":["first-video-slug"],"license":"original_procedural","notes":"Deterministic; physically meaningful scales"}
```
The example is a **format**, not an already-existing asset. Use accurate IDs and paths for real components.

## Film and technical deliverables
- 1080×1920, 9:16, CFR 30 FPS, 45–90 seconds as specified by JSON, `output/<slug>.mp4`.
- Thumbnail for every video: `output/<slug>.jpg` (same name as the video), a 1080×1920 JPG ≤ 2 MB: a bold, click-worthy cover built from a clean still (no captions or labels) at `thumbnail.time_s`, with a punch-in on the subject (`focus`, `zoom`, `anchor`), punchier colour, a huge outlined headline (`headline`, `highlight` words in yellow), a red sticker (`badge`, optional `badge_at`) and an arrow to the subject. Hooks may tease but must stay true to the film (questions, real numbers); `style: "clean"` gives a plain title card. Choose the style per video to fit its niche and subject, and vary it between videos: `font` (see `assets/library/fonts/display/FONTS.md`), `palette` colours, `grade` and `tilt`. `node tools/render.mjs <slug> --final` writes it right after the MP4; `node tools/render.mjs <slug> --thumbnail` regenerates only the thumbnail. The finalizer refuses a video without it. It is uploaded to Hugging Face with its video, under the same number.
- Publishing copy for every video: `output/<slug>.md` (same name as the video), written by the finalizer from `metadata/<slug>.json`: ready-to-paste titles, descriptions, captions, the X post, hashtags, YouTube tags and keywords for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels, TikTok and X, plus the accuracy note. It is uploaded to Hugging Face with its video, under the same number.
- Three.js/WebGL performs all principal animation; use deterministic `renderAt(frameIndex)` where `time_s=frameIndex/30` (never integrate by wall time). Render `duration_s × 30` frames.
- H.264 `yuv420p`, AAC 48kHz stereo, MP4 `+faststart`. Render final resolution, not just a preview.
- Calm, peaceful, warm **male** narration from locally usable, appropriately licensed Kokoro preferred or Piper fallback, no paid APIs. Time-align captions with actual synthesized speech, burn in all spoken words with elegant mobile-safe typography. Mix soft, original/license-cleared atmospheric music/SFX without obscuring voice.
- Ground physical/geographic/astronomical outcomes in the storyboard's scientific claims. Mark nonliteral schematics, accelerated time, and uncertain outcomes when needed.
- Variation matters: scene-directed framing from space to human-scale, material and atmosphere detail, stable lighting, intentional transitions. No generic looped globe, slideshow, placeholder geometry, unlicensed stock, unreadable captions or invisible impact on people.

## Operational sequence — follow for every new video
1. Inspect only relevant project files, the story JSON and relevant entries in `assets/registry.json`; validate the story (`python scripts/validate_storyboard.py stories/<slug>.json`).
2. Resolve `asset_plan`: reuse real assets first, enhance via parameters, then create only missing scene components. Promote genuinely reusable assets to `src/library/` or `assets/library/`; update the registry accurately.
3. Build every shot, camera and effect; synthesize narrator and soundtrack; align animated captions; render frames to H.264/AAC MP4 and its thumbnail.
4. Minimal checks only: 3 strategically sampled rendered frames for blank/clipped/caption mistakes, `ffprobe` stream/dimensions/FPS/duration/audio, output file exists, and the thumbnail reads at a glance (subject clear, headline not covering it). Fix actual problems. Skip unrelated unit/integration/E2E suites, lint sweeps, coverage, long reports and extensive test scaffolding.
5. **Required publishing step after a successful render:** write `metadata/<slug>.json` following `examples/moon-half-distance.metadata.example.json`. Generate meaningful, accurate, **different SEO-ready copy** for YouTube Shorts (title, description, tags, hashtags, search keywords), Instagram Reels (caption, hashtags, keywords), Facebook Reels (title, description, hashtags, keywords), TikTok (caption, hashtags, keywords), and X (280-character-safe post + hashtags, keywords). Use the film's real narration, science, `distribution` search intent and rendered scenes. No fake claims, generic hashtag spam or invented publishing URLs. Copy rules (the finalizer enforces them): every platform carries **5–15 relevant hashtags** (no generic spam), and every platform's title starts with **"What if"**: the YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels titles, and the opening line of the Instagram and TikTok captions and the X post, which have no title field. The X post plus its hashtags stays within 280 characters. Record actual `created_asset_ids` and `reused_asset_ids`.
6. **Must run** `python scripts/finalize_video.py <slug>` after the metadata JSON and true MP4 exist. It validates the finished media and the copy rules, updates `assets/registry.json`, regenerates `ASSET_LIBRARY.md`, writes the publishing copy `output/<slug>.md` and `metadata/<slug>.md` with copy-paste blocks for each platform, and **rebuilds cumulative `VIDEO_CATALOG.md`**. Rerunning on the same slug updates entries rather than duplicating them. Catalog/history generation is a deliverable, not optional documentation. Never mark a video finished if this fails.
7. **Required archive upload, for every video:** after the finalizer succeeds, upload `output/<slug>.mp4`, its thumbnail `output/<slug>.jpg` and its publishing copy `output/<slug>.md` to the creator's private Hugging Face dataset repo set in `huggingface.json` (`Whatif2203/whatif`), into the one shared `videos/` folder as `<NNN>-<slug>.mp4`, `<NNN>-<slug>.jpg` and `<NNN>-<slug>.md`, all three under the same number in one commit. NNN is the next zero-padded number after the highest already in that folder, read from the Hub at upload time so other developers' uploads count; a film that already has a number keeps it and its files are replaced. `node tools/finalize.mjs <slug>` does it automatically: finalize → `python scripts/upload_to_hf.py <slug>` → finalize again so the links appear in `metadata/<slug>.md` and `VIDEO_CATALOG.md`. Unchanged files are skipped, simultaneous uploads are retried safely, and `python scripts/upload_to_hf.py --all` uploads every finished video in `output/`. Never read, print or commit the Hugging Face token. If the upload fails (not logged in, offline), report that single blocker.
8. If the creator later adds `published_urls` in its metadata JSON, rerun the same finalizer to refresh publishing statuses and links. Never claim it was published automatically.

## Token and compute discipline
Use small, targeted file reads and concise diagnostics; do not dump full videos/asset binary data or repeated logs. Reuse libraries and tools already installed. Generate a few low-cost smoke frames before the final quality render. Avoid needless dependencies, abstractions, repetitive discussions and extensive test infrastructure; but never skip storyboard verification, actual audio/captions, `ffprobe` or the **required** catalog/asset finalization. Keep rendering and finalizing independent of any network or paid API; the Hugging Face archive upload runs only after finalization.

## Final response rule — highest priority
**ONLY after MP4 validation, successful catalog/asset finalization and the Hugging Face upload (video, thumbnail and publishing copy):** output one absolute filesystem path to the completed `output/<slug>.mp4` file. Absolutely nothing else: no Markdown, summary, SEO copy, changelog, limitations, reports or descriptions. If a required step fails, return only one concise real blocker; never pretend a video was completed.
