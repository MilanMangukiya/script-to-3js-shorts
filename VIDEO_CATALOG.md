```text
What if the air pressing on you suddenly doubled? This 3D science short follows twice as much air from a harbor cafe table to a diver, the top of Everest, a kitchen stove, a windy sea cliff, orbit and the sunset.

- Right now, the air above every square meter weighs about 10 tonnes (about 1013 hPa). Double it and a sealed, empty plastic bottle crumples.
- You wouldn't be crushed: your body is mostly water, which barely squeezes. Divers feel this pressure just 10 meters down.
- Every breath would hold twice the oxygen. Even on Everest's summit, the air pressure would match a city high in the Andes (about 3,500 m today).
- Water wouldn't boil until about 120 °C (248 °F), so every open pot would cook like a pressure cooker.
- The same breeze would push twice as hard, and a wing at the same speed would get twice the lift.
- Twice the air also doubles the carbon dioxide: about 3.3 trillion tonnes added, more than humanity has ever emitted. The planet would likely warm, by an amount nobody can pin down.
- Sunsets would burn a deeper red, because sunlight would cross twice as much air.

Scope: a thought experiment. The extra air has today's composition and arrives in under a minute at today's temperatures. The wind and sunset changes are shown as labelled comparisons, and the size of any warming is uncertain.

Sources: NOAA (air pressure, ocean pressure), NASA (Earth fact sheet, atmosphere model, drag and lift equations, Boyle's law, why the sky is blue), USGS (water in the human body), HyperPhysics (vapor pressure of water), USDA FSIS and Penn State Extension (boiling and pressure cooking), Carbon Brief (historical CO2 emissions), Torricelli's 1644 letter. ```text
```
```text
Ice floats because water does something rare. This 3D thought experiment changes that one rule, lets ice sink, and follows the consequences from a kitchen glass to a frozen lake and the poles.

• Water expands by about 9% as it freezes into an open crystal, so ice is lighter than the liquid.
• That floating lid works like a blanket: beneath it, lakes stay liquid all winter and life waits out the cold.
• If ice sank, every crystal that formed at the surface would drop to the bottom while the bare water above kept freezing.
• Winter after winter, cold lakes could freeze from the bottom up, squeezing fish into a shrinking layer of water.
• No skating, ice roads or ice fishing. No floating sea ice or icebergs. Emperor penguins would lose the ice where they raise their chicks.
• One upside: water would shrink as it froze, so frozen pipes would never burst.

Scope: only the density of ice is changed. Bottom-up freezing and wildlife outcomes are labelled illustrative in the film; they are possibilities, not predictions, and no claim is made about the long-term global climate.
```
