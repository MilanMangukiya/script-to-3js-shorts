```text
What if the air pressing on you suddenly doubled? This 3D science short follows twice as much air from a harbor cafe table to a diver, the top of Everest, a kitchen stove, a windy sea cliff, orbit and the sunset.

- Right now, the air above every square meter weighs about 10 tonnes (about 1013 hPa). Double it and a sealed, empty plastic bottle crumples.
- You wouldn't be crushed: your body is mostly water, which barely squeezes. Divers feel this pressure just 10 meters down.
- Every breath would hold twice the oxygen. Even on Everest's summit, the air pressure would match a city high in the Andes (about 3,500 m today).
- Water wouldn't boil until about 120 °C (248 °F), so every open pot would cook like a pressure cooker.
- The same breeze would push twice as hard, and a wing at the same speed would get twice the lift.
- Twice the air also doubles the carbon dioxide: about 3.3 trillion tonnes added, more than humanity has ever emitted. The planet would likely warm, by an amount nobody can pin down.
- Sunsets would burn a deeper red, because sunlight would cross twice as much air.

Scope: a thought experiment. The extra air has today's composition and arrives in under a minute at today's temperatures. The wind and sunset changes are shown as labelled comparisons, and the size of any warming is uncertain.
```
