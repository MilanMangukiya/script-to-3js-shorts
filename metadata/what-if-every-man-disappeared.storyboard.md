**Core question:** What would happen to people and to nature if every man and boy on Earth vanished in a single instant?

**Viewer takeaway:** Humanity would survive, grieving and smaller, while oceans and cities briefly fell quiet; men would return within nine months as tens of millions of baby boys, and twenty years later the oldest man alive would be twenty.


## Scientific model

**Baseline reality:** The world of 2026 (UN World Population Prospects 2024, medium projection): about 8.30 billion people, about 4.17 billion male and 4.13 billion female; about 132 million births a year with roughly 105 boys born per 100 girls. ILO modelled estimates put employment at about 70% of men and 46% of women aged 15+, so men hold about 60% of jobs. Women are about 67% of health and care workers but were only about 1.3% of seafarers in 2021. Traffic, shipping and industry are major sources of noise and vibration that wildlife measurably responds to.

**Counterfactual change:** At one instant (T0) every living person counted as male in UN population statistics (men and boys of all ages, about 4.17 billion people) vanishes painlessly. Bodies and worn clothing disappear; anything held, carried or being operated stays behind. Nothing else changes: women and girls, pregnancies already underway (whatever the baby's sex), frozen sperm, eggs and embryos, all animals and all infrastructure remain.

### Assumptions

- 'Men and boys' means the UN 'male' category used for demographic arithmetic; the film does not model gender identity and acknowledges that intersex and transgender people exist.

- The removal is instantaneous, painless and one-time; no reversal, replacement population or new reproductive technology is assumed.

- Pregnancies already underway continue regardless of the baby's sex; frozen sperm, eggs and embryos in banks and clinics are unaffected.

- All non-human animals, including male animals, are unaffected.

- Infrastructure is physically intact at T0: lights and automated systems keep running until they fail, run out of fuel or are shut down, so city lights stay on in every post-T0 shot.

- Secondary casualties from accidents and failures in the first hours are acknowledged in the model but neither quantified nor depicted.

- Story-world times (T0, 'overnight', 'weeks later', '+20 years', '+40 years') are labeled on screen wherever the picture jumps in time.

- Real-world analogs (Bay of Fundy after 9/11 in 2001; COVID-19 lockdowns in 2020) are presented as measured evidence of how the ground and wildlife respond to less human activity, not as predictions of exact magnitudes in this scenario.

### Causal chain

- T0: about 4.17 billion people vanish; humanity falls to about 4.13 billion, roughly the size of the world in 1975.

- About 60% of the employed workforce is gone at once, very unevenly: health and care keep most staff (about 67% women) while cargo shipping loses almost all crews (about 1% women).

- Within hours to days many vehicles, ships, machines and industrial processes stop or are shut down, while lights and automated systems keep running for a while.

- Human-made noise and vibration drop sharply; measured analogs show ground vibration quieting by up to 50% (2020) and ship noise falling about 6 dB (2001).

- Wildlife responds quickly: right whale stress-hormone levels fell with less ship noise, and city sparrows sang softer yet were heard about twice as far.

- Births continue for nine months from pregnancies already underway: about 99 million babies, about 50 million of them boys.

- Afterward births plunge, sustained mainly by frozen sperm, until the post-T0 boys grow up; twenty years on, the oldest man alive is about twenty.

- Societies grieve, retrain and rebuild; activity and noise return over years, and over decades street life could look ordinary again.

### Uncertainties

- How fast survivors restore transport, energy, food supply and shipping, which sets how long the quiet lasts.

- Secondary casualties and infrastructure failures in the first hours and days.

- The share of pregnancies carried to term under extreme shock and disrupted care (the 50 million estimate would fall).

- The size and usability of global frozen-sperm stocks and how quickly insemination and IVF capacity could scale.

- Large regional variation in workforce shares (women's share of health workers, for example, differs widely by country and role).

- The wildlife evidence comes from single regions and species (Bay of Fundy right whales; San Francisco Bay Area white-crowned sparrows); responses elsewhere may differ.

- Social, economic and political outcomes, and the population path after twenty years, are not modeled.

## Creative direction

**Logline:** In one silent second every man and boy on Earth vanishes. Following the sound of a changed planet, from a half-empty crosswalk to quiet seas and singing sparrows, the film finds that humanity survives and that men return within nine months as baby boys.

**Visual identity:** Premium natural-history realism for every place (city, port, interiors, ocean, nursery), intercut with restrained, clearly tagged glass-and-light schematics for numbers (ribbon chart, token meter, seismic trace, glass population pyramid). Recurring motif: human activity drawn as pale sound rings. Engine rings shrink as the world quiets; birdsong and whale-call rings grow. People are stylised, individuated figures (humanoid-figure-v1) seen at a respectful distance; absence is shown through empty chairs and left-behind objects, never bodies. No gender color-coding (no pink or blue) and no costume stereotypes; age and individuality come from proportions, posture, gait and accessories.

### Palette

- #0E1B33 blue-hour navy (skies, night)

- #2B4A73 dusk blue (atmosphere, shadows)

- #E8A55A sodium amber (practical lights)

- #F3E6C8 warm bone (glass schematics, labels)

- #1F5F63 Fundy teal (ocean)

- #7D8287 slate grey (machines, concrete)

- #F2B66D caption accent amber

- #CFE3EA pale cyan (sound rings)

**Lighting:** Motivated, physically plausible light only. City: blue-hour skylight plus 3000 K street lamps, warm 2700–3500 K windows and vehicle practicals. Orbit: dusk sunlight on the left of the limb, Black Marble night lights on the right. Interiors: dimmed 4500 K hospital panels with one warm desk lamp; red low-level light and screen glow on the ship's bridge. Ocean: sun shafts through a Snell window and green-blue fog. Sparrow: soft morning sun. Ending: dawn, then the time-lapse back to blue hour. Electricity stays on after T0 because lighting is automated, so lit windows and streetlights remain in every post-T0 city shot. Scene-referred HDR with ACES tone mapping (film-pipeline-v1); bloom only on true light sources; no fake lens flares.

**Camera language:** Unhurried documentary camera on camera-keyframe-rig-v1: slow push-ins, lateral trucks, one crane-up from street to orbit and one continuous pull-back from a nursery to the city. Lenses: 12–22° vertical FOV for intimate telephoto (sparrow, newborn, port), 28–40° for human-scale scenes, 44–50° underwater. No handheld shake and no whip pans. Ease semantics: each keyframe's ease shapes the segment that ends at it; two-keyframe shots repeat the same ease on both keys, and holds are authored as two identical keyframes, so either rig convention gives the intended motion. The last keyframe of S10 SH02 is identical to the first keyframe of S01 SH01, closing the loop.

**Motion language:** Everything is a pure function of absolute time (deterministic_frame_by_frame). Motion is slow and organic: walking at 1.25–1.45 m/s with analytic gait phase; machines decelerate along smooth envelopes rather than stopping dead; whales fluke about every 4 s; sound rings expand at a visual 6–40 m/s and fade with spherical spreading. Data schematics animate with ease_in_out_cubic over 0.5–1.2 s and then hold long enough to read. Time-lapses keep any light oscillation at or below 1.5 Hz with motion blur, so nothing strobes.

**World scale policy:** Real scale by default: 1 unit = 1 m in city, port, interior, sparrow and underwater shots; 1 unit = 1,000 km for Earth (radius 6.371). Earth is never enlarged and the Sun stays off-frame. Reference sizes: right whale adult 14 m and calf 5 m; white-crowned sparrow 0.16 m; container ship 300 m; ship-to-shore gantry crane 55 m; newborn hand about 6 cm. Deliberate deviations are tagged on screen: the port is filmed as an ILLUSTRATIVE tilt-shift diorama; the ribbon chart, token meter, seismic trace, sound rings, ground cross-section, birth glints and glass pyramid are SCHEMATIC or ILLUSTRATIVE; underwater visibility (about 35–45 m) is illustrative; song-ring radii are illustrative.

### Emotional arc

- Stillness and disbelief (0–12 s): a crosswalk half-empties without a sound

- Understanding (12–26 s): why the world would go quiet, and proof that it can

- Grief with dignity (26–36 s): absence in nearly every home while care continues

- Unexpected wonder (36–49 s): whales and sparrows answer the quiet

- Hope (49–66 s): men return as baby boys and a new generation rises

- Resolution and loop (66–75 s): grief, rebuilding and an ordinary street again

**Opening hook:** Frame 0 is an ordinary, busy crosswalk at blue hour just after rain. At 0.9 s about half the pedestrians, including two children, fade in place; an umbrella drops, a walked bicycle tips over, and the survivors slow and look around. The spoken and captioned question lands by 3.05 s: 'What if every man on Earth disappeared?' No logo, greeting or call to action.

**Ending payoff:** A clear answer: humanity survives, smaller, grieving and rebuilding, while nature gets a temporary quiet. A curbside memorial and a women's repair crew at dawn give way to a decades-long time-lapse in which the crosswalk fills with a new, mixed generation: 'And one day, a busy street could look ordinary again.'
